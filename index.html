How it's measured. This month = change from the last close of the previous month to the latest close. "Falling since high" counts trading days since the 52-week closing high. "Last up week" is the most recent completed week (Friday close) that finished above the week before; the current week is shown separately because it isn't finished. Down-week streak = consecutive completed down weeks. The weekly chart runs oldest to newest, with gains above the line and losses below; the dashed last bar is the week still in progress.
Reasons come from news coverage around each stock's biggest down days this month. High confidence = a specific, reported catalyst. Medium = a sector or theme move with supporting coverage. Low = no single catalyst found.
Winners = the 50 S&P 500 members with the biggest gain this month, measured the same way. Volatility = how much a stock's daily price swings, as the annualized standard deviation of daily returns over the last 30 trading days (1-year volatility uses the past year). A stock at 60% swings roughly three times as much as one at 20%. Average daily move is the typical size of one day's move, up or down. Beta compares a stock's daily moves with the S&P 500's over the past year: 1.0 moves with the market, 2.0 moves about twice as much.
Blue chips = the 50 largest S&P 500 companies by market cap (one share class per company). Analyst upside = average price target vs. last close.
Data: Yahoo Finance daily closes (split-adjusted) for all 503 S&P 500 members, plus Yahoo quote data for valuation and analyst targets. Rankings and reasons refresh every weekday after the close (about 5:30pm ET). Prices on the cards, the blue-chip tiles and the S&P figure update live from Finnhub during market hours, roughly every minute per stock (faster for the stocks on your screen); % this month, % off the high and this week's bar recalculate from the live price.
